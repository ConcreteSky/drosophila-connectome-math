Abstract

This report investigates whether the complete synaptic wiring diagram of an adult Drosophila melanogaster brain can function as a biological computing substrate for discrete logic and arithmetic when trained exclusively with scalar reward (+1) and punishment (−1) signals. We ingest the 2024 FlyWire v783 whole-brain connectome (138,639 proofread neurons, 15,091,983 directed edges, and 54,492,922 weighted synapses) into DuckDB and Parquet, compile it into a Dale's-Law-compliant Compressed Sparse Row (CSR) PyTorch representation, and simulate batched Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) dynamics across local CPU and GPU backends. At batch size 1, sparse GPU simulation achieves a 12.1× speedup over CPU (129.40 ms vs. 1572.10 ms per 60 ms trial) within a 330.08 MB memory footprint. Under three-factor dopamine-modulated Reward-STDP (R-STDP) and REINFORCE baselines, the connectome reaches 70.0% held-out accuracy on delayed_xor (versus 40.0% for a matched PyTorch GRU) and exhibits functional collapse (0.0%) followed by plastic recovery (100.0%) under Central Complex ablation. However, scaling to bit-coded 0–100 arithmetic with strict train/test isolation (800 sampled pairs, 200 unseen pairs) reveals an honest scientific boundary: while per-bit unseen accuracy rises above chance (62.8% for addition, 67.3% for subtraction), exact 9-bit integer generalization remains low (1.0%–2.5%), demonstrating that a biological reservoir with a single plastic readout cannot replace multi-layer carry-propagation circuits.

1.Introduction

Conventional artificial neural networks rely on uniform, dense tensor architectures trained via global error backpropagation. By contrast, biological brains operate through sparse, heterogeneous, sign-constrained graphs shaped by evolution. With the release of the FlyWire v783 whole-brain connectome of Drosophila melanogaster, it is now possible to instantiate every proofread neuron and synaptic connection of an adult animal brain in silico. Rather than treating the connectome as a static graph or turning the application into a hardcoded calculator, this project asks a concrete empirical question: to what extent can a real insect connectome solve working-memory logic and arithmetic tasks solely through biologically plausible reward (+1) and punishment (−1) plasticity?

To answer this rigorously without cherry-picking or memorization, we implement an end-to-end scientific pipeline comprising: (1) reproducible DuckDB/Parquet data ingestion from FlyWire v783, (2) a batched sparse CSR LIF simulator respecting Dale's Law, (3) dual plasticity engines (three-factor dopamine R-STDP and spiking REINFORCE) evaluated against degree-preserving shuffled connectomes and matched-budget PyTorch GRUs, (4) targeted neuropil and hub lesion ablations, and (5) a same-origin Vercel web interface providing real-time 2D anatomical spike visualization and interactive feedback.

3.Mathematical Formulation

3.1 Batched Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) Dynamics

We simulate neural activity at discrete step Δt = 1.0 ms over a T = 60 ms trial window for batch size B. Synaptic currents decay exponentially with τsyn = 5.0 ms (β = e−Δt/τsyn), while membrane potentials integrate toward resting potential Vrest = −52.0 mV with τm = 20.0 ms (α = e−Δt/τm):

Isyn(t) = β Isyn(t−1) + WeffT s(t−1) + Iext(t) (1)
V(t) = α V(t−1) + (1 − α)(Vrest + Rm Isyn(t)),   s(t) = 1[V(t) ≥ −45.0 mV] (2)

Neurons that fire reset immediately to Vreset = −52.0 mV and enter a 2 ms refractory period. An active binary lesion_mask clamps silenced neurons at Vrest and zeroes their spike output, enabling clean regional and hub ablations.

3.2 Three-Factor Dopamine R-STDP & Dale's Law

Plastic synapses targeting the Mushroom Body and Motor/Output populations maintain decaying pre-synaptic (xi) and post-synaptic (yj) traces alongside a per-synapse eligibility trace eij(t) (λe = 0.95, A+ = 1.0, A− = 0.5). At trial end, global dopamine reward r ∈ {+1, −1} centered by exponential moving baseline r̄ ← 0.95r̄ + 0.05r modulates synaptic weights:

eij(t+1) = λe eij(t) + A+ xi(t) sj(t) − A− si(t) yj(t),    ΔWij = η (r − r̄) eij(T) (3)

To preserve biological validity, Dale's Law is strictly enforced after every update: cholinergic excitatory weights are clamped to [0, wmax] and GABA/glutamatergic inhibitory weights to [−wmax, 0]. A second plasticity engine, spiking REINFORCE, updates readout policy parameters via ∇θ log πθ(y|x)(r − r̄) as an RL baseline.

4.Experimental Design & Scientific Controls

To isolate the contribution of real connectome topology and prevent data leakage, all tasks enforce deterministic train/test splits and are compared against two structural/algorithmic controls:

  • Degree-Preserving Shuffled Brain: Configuration-model rewiring permutes presynaptic column indices across the CSR matrix while preserving every neuron's exact in-degree, out-degree, and E/I sign ratio.
  • Matched-Budget PyTorch GRU: A non-spiking recurrent baseline trained with REINFORCE on identical 60 ms temporal input sequences and episode budgets.
  • Task Suite: (a) delayed_xor with Operand A in [0, 15] ms, silent working-memory delay in [15, 30] ms, Operand B in [30, 45] ms, and readout in [45, 60] ms; (b) mod_add ((a + b) mod 7, 37 train / 12 unseen test); and (c) 0–100 bit-coded addition and subtraction using 14 dual-rail input channels per operand and 9 competing binary output bits (800 sampled training pairs, 200 unseen test pairs).

5.2 Control Baselines & Lesion Recovery (Delayed XOR)

In the 10-episode local smoke experiment on delayed_xor, the real FlyWire connectome with R-STDP achieved 70.0% held-out test accuracy (955.6 mean spikes/trial), outperforming both spiking REINFORCE (60.0%) and the matched PyTorch GRU (40.0%). Silencing the Central Complex immediately collapsed held-out accuracy to 0.0%, whereas continued R-STDP training post-lesion recovered accuracy to 100.0% (938.0 spikes/trial). Silencing the top 5% highest-degree hub neurons eliminated recurrent cascades (0.0 mean spikes).

5.3 Generalization Stress Test on 0–100 Arithmetic

Expanding operands to 0–100 (10,201 possible pairs) with 1,024 Mushroom Body Kenyon-cell projections trained on 800 sampled pairs and evaluated on 200 completely unseen pairs exposes a fundamental distinction between reservoir memorization and algorithmic generalization:

Key Finding: Unseen per-bit accuracy on 0–100 arithmetic reaches 62.8%–67.3% (well above the 50.0% random-bit baseline), confirming that the spiking reservoir extracts real bit-level structure. However, because exact 9-bit integer accuracy requires all nine bits to be simultaneously correct, single-layer R-STDP cannot generalize deep multi-bit carry chains to unseen numbers.

6.Production Deployment & Interactive Visualization

To deploy the application on Vercel without exceeding the 500 MB serverless function limit (which precludes PyTorch and CUDA runtimes), we export the induced 3,000-landmark dorsal subnetwork (61,453 directed edges) and trained 1,024-Kenyon-cell weights into artifacts/serverless/subnetwork.npz (4.13 MB) and layout_and_summary.json (290.5 KB). The FastAPI serverless backend (api/index.py) executes pure-NumPy np.bincount CSR matrix-vector multiplication, strict Pydantic v2 range validation (returning HTTP 422 on out-of-range inputs), and live reward/punishment updates that visibly shift subsequent predictions.

The React + TypeScript frontend organizes the workflow into three progressive-disclosure tabs: (1) Play & Train (live equation banner, spinner-free 0–100 keyboard inputs, 60 FPS anatomical neuropil Canvas with glowing spike trails and dopamine/punishment ripples, top-4 candidate votes, and cumulative Total Weight Shift), (2) Brain Damage Lab (lesion toggles with grayscale/crimson cross-hatching and before/after comparison), and (3) Science & Benchmarks (control tables and DuckDB region statistics).

7.Limitations & Conclusion

This study models neurons as single-compartment LIF units without dendritic nonlinearities, biophysical ion channels, or multi-layer surrogate backpropagation across the 138,639-neuron graph. Moreover, the live serverless deployment runs a 3,000-neuron subnetwork rather than the full GPU connectome. Within these boundaries, the project establishes a reproducible, strictly honest scientific testbed: a real Drosophila connectome accelerated 12.1× on GPU can learn small discrete associations via dopamine R-STDP and recover from targeted lesions, while clearly revealing where biological reservoir computing diverges from exact digital arithmetic.
